A budget story from the PAB minutes

Two rupees a day

What it costs to teach five crore children to read. Every figure on this page is read directly from the Government of India's Project Approval Board minutes for the NIPUN Bharat mission.

₹4,195.73 Cr
approved for 2026-27
For the 2026-27 school year, the Project Approval Board approved ₹4,195.73 crore for NIPUN Bharat, India's national mission for foundational literacy and numeracy. A number that big is hard to feel. So divide it.
Spread it across the 36 states and union territories whose plans the Board approved and it is about ₹116.5 crore each. Still abstract.
Divide by the 5.2 crore children the approved activities reach and the mission budget becomes ₹804 per child for the entire year.
Divide by 365 days and you arrive at the real scale of this mission. ₹2.20 per child per day to teach a generation to read, write and count.
A cup of roadside chai costs about ₹10. The mission's daily budget for a child is less than a quarter of a chai.
The scale

Small rupees, enormous reach

The daily figure is tiny because the denominator is vast. In 2026-27 the approved plans put teaching and learning materials in the hands of 5.2 crore children and equip 24.3 lakh teachers across every state and union territory of India.

How big is that against the country as a whole? Six approval cycles

The budget doubled in six years

From its launch year to 2026-27, the mission's target year, the Board's approved outlay grew from ₹2,059 crore to ₹4,196 crore. The jump after 2024-25 is not simply more of the same money. From 2025-26 the approvals fold in large support for pre-primary Balvatika classes, and the materials budget narrows its focus to the youngest cohort, pre-primary to Grade 2.

Follow the rupee

Where each rupee goes

Take one rupee of the approved budget and split it into its 100 paise. For most of the mission's life, roughly 80 paise of every rupee went straight into children's hands as teaching and learning materials. Watch the split shift as you scroll.

2021-22
0 paise100 paise
In 2021-22, the launch year, 80 paise of every rupee bought learning materials for six crore children in Grades 1 to 5. Teacher training took 12 paise.
In 2022-23 materials peaked at 88 paise in the rupee, the purest year of the books-in-hands strategy.
Through 2023-24 and 2024-25 the Board pushed more into teacher capacity building and independent assessments of learning.
In 2025-26 the split transformed. Nearly half of every rupee now supports pre-primary Balvatika classes, and the materials budget concentrates on pre-primary to Grade 2.
The 2026-27 plan holds that shape. 48 paise for materials, 38 paise for pre-primary support, 11 paise for teacher capacity. The mission's last approved rupee is split between books, Balvatikas and teachers.

36 states, one mission

Small states pay more per child

The same mission costs very different amounts per child depending on where the child lives. Uttar Pradesh reaches 1.3 crore children at ₹1.82 a day. Ladakh, with 5,500 children spread across high Himalayan valleys, spends ₹33.94 a day per child. Fixed costs like project units and training infrastructure do not shrink with enrolment.

The negotiation

What states asked for, and what they got

A PAB approval is the end of a negotiation. Most 2026-27 proposals came through nearly whole. West Bengal's did not. It asked for ₹95.7 crore and was approved ₹63.5 crore, a cut of a third.

How these numbers were made

  • Source. Minutes of the Project Approval Board of the Department of School Education and Literacy, Ministry of Education, for annual plans 2021-22 through 2026-27. Figures are the NIPUN Bharat activity lines of each state's approved annexure, transcribed and reconciled against the totals printed in the documents themselves. All 202 documents that print a NIPUN subtotal reconcile against it.
  • Children and teachers covered. Denominators come from the approvals, not external enrolment data. Children covered is the approved physical count on teaching learning material lines. Teachers covered is the approved count on teacher training and resource material lines. They mean children and teachers reached by those activities, which is smaller than total enrolment.
  • Per-day figures. Calendar-year basis, 365 days. On a school-day basis of roughly 220 days, the 2026-27 headline figure would be about ₹3.65 per child per day.
  • The 2025-26 shift. From 2025-26 the approvals fold pre-primary Balvatika support into the NIPUN block and the materials line narrows to the pre-primary to Grade 2 cohort. Per child figures before and after that year are not directly comparable, which is why the spike in the trend chart is annotated rather than smoothed.
  • Coverage gaps. A few state-years print financial approvals without physical counts. Per child figures for those states are shown as not printed rather than estimated.
  • The UDISE+ comparison. The Foundational-stage enrolment figure in "The scale" section is drawn from the Government of India's own UDISE+ 2023-24 report (Table 2.1, Ministry of Education, Department of School Education & Literacy), the most recent year it has published. It is an independent enrolment count, not the same denominator as "children covered" elsewhere on this page, which stays approval-scope from the PAB minutes, and it is a different year (2023-24) from the 2026-27 approvals it is compared against.
